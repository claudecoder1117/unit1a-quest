## 0. Vocabulary that the doc says is "important" (must be covered)
Point, line, plane, collinear, coplanar, segment, ray, opposite rays.
Angle, side, vertex, acute, straight, right, obtuse, angle bisector, complementary, supplementary, complement, supplement, adjacent, linear pair, vertical angles.
Also NOTATION: how notation for "line" and for "segment" differ, how to read it, how to use it.
  - line AB: AB with a double-arrow over it (↔ overline). Extends forever both ways.
  - segment AB: AB with a plain bar over it. Two endpoints. "AB" with no bar = the LENGTH of segment AB (a number).
  - ray AB: AB with an arrow over it pointing right; endpoint is the FIRST letter (A). Ray AB ≠ ray BA.
  - opposite rays: two rays with a common endpoint that form a line (e.g. ray FA and ray FD when F is between A and D).
  - angle: ∠ABC — vertex is the MIDDLE letter (B). m∠ABC = the measure (a number of degrees). ∠ABC ≅ ∠DEF means congruent (same measure); m∠ABC = m∠DEF means measures equal.
  - plane: named by a capital script letter or by 3 non-collinear points (plane ABC).
Standard definitions (textbook-standard, use these):
  - Point: a location, no size. Line: straight, extends forever in both directions, contains infinitely many points; through any two points there is exactly one line.
  - Plane: flat surface extending forever in all directions; through any three non-collinear points there is exactly one plane.
  - Collinear: points on the same line. Coplanar: points/lines in the same plane.
  - Segment: part of a line with two endpoints. Ray: part of a line with one endpoint, extends forever one way.
  - Angle: two rays (sides) with a common endpoint (vertex).
  - Acute: 0° < m < 90°. Right: exactly 90°. Obtuse: 90° < m < 180°. Straight: exactly 180°.
  - Angle bisector: a ray that divides an angle into two congruent angles.
  - Complementary: two angles whose measures sum to 90°. Complement of x = 90 − x.
  - Supplementary: two angles whose measures sum to 180°. Supplement of x = 180 − x.
  - Adjacent angles: two angles that share a vertex and a side but no interior points.
  - Linear pair: two adjacent angles whose non-common sides are opposite rays (they are supplementary).
  - Vertical angles: two non-adjacent angles formed by two intersecting lines (they are congruent).
  - Intersection facts: two distinct lines intersect in at most one point; two distinct planes intersect in a line; a line and a plane intersect in a point, or the line lies in the plane, or they don't intersect.

## 1. "Angles Practice" review sheet (pages 13–14 of the packet) — with teacher key
Figure F1 (Warm-up and #10): Two lines cross at F. Line AD is horizontal (A/G on the left, D on the right). Line EC goes from lower-left E through F to upper-right C. Ray FB goes straight up from F, with a right-angle mark between FB and FD (so ∠BFD = 90°, and ∠BFA = 90°). In the warm-up version the left point is labeled G; in #10 it's labeled A.
Warm-up (identify from figure F1 with left point G): 
  - 3 pairs of supplementary angles: e.g. ∠EFG & ∠GFC, ∠GFC & ∠CFD, ∠AFC & ∠CFD, ∠BFC & ∠CFD? (No: ∠BFC + ∠CFD = 90.) Valid: any linear pair: (∠EFG, ∠GFC), (∠GFC, ∠CFD), (∠CFD, ∠DFE), (∠DFE, ∠EFG), (∠BFG, ∠BFD) etc. Teacher's key lists ∠EFA+∠AFC, ∠AFC+∠CFD, ∠AFE+∠EFD (teacher used A for the left point).
  - 1 pair of complementary angles: ∠BFC & ∠CFD (they sum to 90 since ∠BFD = 90). Also ∠GFE & ∠EF? no. Teacher: ∠BFC + ∠CFD. Non-adjacent complementary pair also valid: ∠BFC & ∠AFE (vertical to ∠CFD).
  - 1 pair of vertical angles: ∠AFE & ∠CFD (or ∠AFC & ∠DFE).
  - 2 linear pairs: (∠AFC, ∠CFD), (∠AFE, ∠EFD), (∠GFB, ∠BFD) etc.
  - 2 non-examples of adjacent angles: teacher gave ∠BFA & ∠CFD (no common side), ∠BFC & ∠AFE (non-adjacent complementary).
#2 One of two complementary angles is twice the other. Find the measures. → x + 2x = 90, x = 30. Answer: 30° and 60°.
#3 One of two supplementary angles is 70° greater than the second. Find the larger. → x + (x+70) = 180, x = 55. Larger = 125°.
#4 Figure F2: Triangle ACE with apex C at top, A bottom-left, E bottom-right. B is on side AC, D is on side CE, segment BD drawn. Printed labels: CB = 3m + 4, BA = n − 1, DE = m² − 6, AE = 8. (CD has NO printed label — the '19' seen in the key is the teacher's derived value; there are no tick marks printed.) Given AC ≅ CE and BD bisects AC and CE (so B and D are midpoints). Find m, n, and perimeter of triangle ACE.
   Key: m² − 6 = 3m + 4 → m² − 3m − 10 = 0 → (m−5)(m+2)=0 → m = 5 (m = −2 rejected: negative side length). Then CB = 19, so n − 1 = 19 → n = 20. AC = CE = 38, AE = 8, perimeter = 84.
#5 m∠MAH = x² + 3, m∠HAC = 11 − 7x, m∠MAC = 6 − 16x. Ray AH is between rays AM and AC. Does AH bisect ∠MAC? Explain.
   Key: x² + 3 + 11 − 7x = 6 − 16x → x² + 9x + 8 = 0 → (x+8)(x+1) = 0 → x = −8 or x = −1. If x = −8: m∠MAH = 67, m∠HAC = 67 → YES bisects (congruent halves). If x = −1: m∠MAH = 4, m∠HAC = 18 → NO. (Both cases must be discussed.)
#6 The supplement of an angle is 30° less than five times the complement. Find the angle (and its complement and supplement). → 180 − x = 5(90 − x) − 30 → 4x = 240 → x = 60. Angle 60°, complement 30°, supplement 120°.
#7 Two supplementary angles are in the ratio 7:2. Find each. → 9 parts = 180, 20° each part → 140° and 40°.
#8 Ratio of supplement to complement is 5:2. Find the supplement. → (180−x)/(90−x) = 5/2 → 360 − 2x = 450 − 5x → 3x = 90 → x = 30; comp 60, supp 150. Answer: 150°.
#9 Ratio of (angle × supplement) to (angle × complement) is 13:4. Find the angle. → x(180−x)/(x(90−x)) = 13/4 → 720x − 4x² = 1170x − 13x² → 9x² − 450x = 0 → 9x(x − 50) = 0 → x = 50 (x=0 rejected). Angle 50°, comp 40°, supp 130°.
#10 Figure F1 with left point A: ∠BFC = −x + 84 (between ray FB and ray FC), ∠AFE = 2x² − 4x + 3 (below AD, between FA and FE). Right angle at ∠BFD. Find x, m∠CFD, m∠DFE.
   Key: ∠AFE and ∠CFD are vertical → ∠CFD = 2x² − 4x + 3. ∠BFC + ∠CFD = 90 → 2x² − 4x + 3 − x + 84 = 90 → 2x² − 5x − 3 = 0 → (2x+1)(x−3) = 0 → x = −1/2 or x = 3.
   Teacher's key accepts BOTH: x = 3 → m∠CFD = 9°, m∠DFE = 171°; x = −1/2 → m∠CFD = 5.5°, m∠DFE = 174.5°. (Both give positive angles, so both valid.)
#11 Four times the complement of an angle is 12 more than twice the difference between the complement and its supplement. → 4(90 − x) = 12 + 2((180 − x) − (90 − x)) = 12 + 2(90) = 192 → 360 − 4x = 192 → x = 42. Angle 42°, comp 48°, supp 138°.

## 2. Doc problems #5, #6, #7 (figures in the doc) — with teacher key (doc-key images)
Doc #5 (figure doc-fig-5-bisect.png): "If m∠ABC = 11x + 19, does ray BD bisect ∠ABC? Show work and explain why or why not." Figure: vertex B at bottom, ray BA goes up-left, ray BD goes up (slightly right), ray BC goes right. m∠ABD = 5x + 16, m∠DBC = 8x − 23.
   Key: 5x + 16 + 8x − 23 = 11x + 19 → 13x − 7 = 11x + 19 → 2x = 26 → x = 13. m∠ABD = 81°, m∠DBC = 81°. Both 81° so ∠ABD ≅ ∠DBC, so BD bisects ∠ABC. YES.
Doc #6: "The measure of the supplement of an angle is 30 more than three times the complement. Determine the measures of the angles." → 180 − x = 30 + 3(90 − x) → 180 − x = 300 − 3x → 2x = 120 → x = 60. Angle 60°, supplement 120°, complement 30°.
Doc #7 (figure doc-fig-7-lines.png): Two lines intersect. A horizontal line and a line sloping from upper-left to lower-right. Angles: upper-left = 3x + y, upper-right = 4y + x − 5, lower-right = 4x + y + 10. Solve for x, y, and the angle measures.
   Key: vertical angles: 3x + y = 4x + y + 10 → x = −10. Linear pair: (3x + y) + (4y + x − 5) = 180 → 4x + 5y = 185 → −40 + 5y = 185 → y = 45. Angles: 3x + y = 15°, 4y + x − 5 = 165°, 4x + y + 10 = 15°, remaining (lower-left) = 165°.

## 3. "4a. comp supp word problems" (linked Google Doc) — 16 problems, answers in parentheses are the teacher's; worked key images wordprobs-key-p1/p2/p4.png
Instruction: solve by setting up an algebraic equation (no guess and check). x = angle, 90 − x = complement, 180 − x = supplement.
1. The supplement of an angle is 10 more than nine times the angle. What is the angle and its complement? (17°, 73°)  [180−x = 10+9x → x=17]
2. The supplement of an angle less 14 is three times the complement. What is the angle and its supplement? (52°, 128°)  [180−x−14 = 3(90−x) → x=52]
3. An angle plus its complement is 6 more than half of the supplement of the angle. Angle and its complement? (12°, 78°)  [90 = 6 + ½(180−x) → x=12]
4. The measure of an angle is 6 more than twice the measure of its complement. Find the larger of these two angles. (62°)  [x = 6+2(90−x) → x=62; comp 28]
5. Two supplementary angles are in a ratio of 5:7. Find the complement of the smaller angle. (15°)  [smaller = 75, comp 15]
6. One of two complementary angles is 6 less than one-half the other. Find the supplement of the smaller. (154°)  [x = ½(90−x) − 6 → x=26; supp 154]
7. The supplement of an angle less 8 is seven times the angle. Find the supplement of the complement of the angle. (111.5°)  [180−x−8=7x → x=21.5; comp 68.5; supp of that 111.5]
8. Half of the difference between two supplementary angles is 3.5. Find the larger. (93.5°)  [½(180−x−x)=3.5 → x=86.5; larger 93.5]
9. The difference between the supplements of two complementary angles is 24. Find the two complementary angles. (33°, 57°)
10. The ratio of an angle to its supplement is 3:7. Determine the ratio of the angle to its complement. (3:2)  [x=54, comp 36, 54:36 = 3:2]
11. The ratio of the supplement of an angle to its complement is 7:2. What is the supplement of the complement of the angle? (144°)  [x=54, comp 36, supp of 36 = 144]
12. The product of an angle and its complement is 344. What is the supplement of the smaller angle? (176°)  [x(90−x)=344 → x=86 or 4; smaller 4; supp 176]
13. Three times the difference between an angle and 5 is ten less than double the complement. What is the supplement of the angle? (143°)  [3(x−5)=2(90−x)−10 → x=37; supp 143]
14. The ratio of one less than half of the complement of an angle to the angle itself is 1:2. What is the supplement of the complement of the angle? (134°)  [(½(90−x)−1)/x = ½ → x=44; comp 46; supp of 46 = 134]
15. The complement of two more than an angle is one-third of the sum of the angle and its supplement. What is the complement of the original angle? (62°)  [90−(x+2) = ⅓(180) → x=28; comp 62]
16. The ratio of 6 more than an angle to 4 less than the supplement of the complement of the angle is 1:5. What is the complement of the angle? (76°)  [(x+6)/(180−(90−x)−4) = 1/5 → 5x+30 = 86+x → x=14; comp 76]
