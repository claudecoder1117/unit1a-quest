## 1. What was built

### `site/js/page.js` — the three additive functions, beside the composer

The game layer never composes. `composePage` runs first and owns what is studied; these three
functions **partition, price and re-interleave** what it produced, and nothing else.

1. **`composeBundles(save, opts)`** — the posted board. It
   - prices every composed item through `econ.postedFor` (`jobTargetOf`), leaving the queue item
     itself untouched and carried through as `.item` so `run.js` / `card.js` still render it verbatim;
   - picks out the **criticals** (`BOARD`: a due review with `bucket ≤ 2`, or `overdue ≥ 1 day`, or
     the Final-Sweep clamp), most overdue first, capped at the shape's target count;
   - posts `min(5, available)` contracts and narrows the draft through `BOARD.draftFor`;
   - sizes the **pool** so the drafted union lands on `jobBudget(shape).targets`;
   - partitions the ordinary locks by make, handing contracts makes from as many different wings as
     possible, so each contract has a dominant make and the board spans ≥ 3 wings;
   - **replicates every critical into `r = max(3, n − d + 1)` contracts** with a seeded sliding
     window, which is G3.7 proof 7's intersection bound (`r + d − n ≥ 1`) done exactly;
   - repairs wing purity so no wing owns `d` pure contracts — which is what makes a one-wing draft
     impossible (G3.4, G12 #11);
   - returns the per-wing supply from J5b's `wingSupply`.
2. **`draftUnion(bundles, picks, opts)`** — dedupe in board order (a replicated critical is answered
   **once**), then the ramp + the spread in one pass, then **the composer's own `spreadSkills`** as
   the final gate, then composePage's own two ordering laws (a hard lock is never first, a Rematch is
   never item 1). Contracts survive only as `from` / `sources` on the envelope.
3. **`jobBudget(shape)`** — every number recomputed from `data/job.js SHAPES` and
   `econ.shapeTable` / `econ.decisionCount`; nothing transcribed.

**The interleave, stated once.** `arrangeJob` is a greedy that at each beat takes the **lowest-tier**
remaining lock whose make does not open a run of three *and whose removal leaves the rest
arrangeable*. The arrangeability oracle is the standard multiset bound under a run cap `k`:

```
max_s c_s  ≤  k · (total − max_s c_s + 1)  −  (runLen, when the max make is the one already running)
```

Lowest-tier-first **is** the 1→4 ramp; the feasibility filter is what stops the ramp painting the
last three locks into one make. When the two genuinely cannot both hold, `LIMITS.sameSkillRun = 2`
wins (COMPOSED wins on G10 #19) and the result says so in `monotone: false`. Measured: over the
500-case corpus the run cap held **every time** and the ramp was monotone in **every case where a
monotone run-safe order exists at all** (§4 row 4).

### `site/js/job/board.js` — the board around the partition

- `x2Marks(dateISO, jobIndex, targets)` — the day's ×2 vector, an independent `p = 1/6` per target
  seeded `cyrb53(dateISO|jobIndex|targetIndex)` exactly as G3.6 writes it.
- `jobIndexFor` / `jobSeedFor` — the pinned job seed (G3.7 proof 6: a resume may not re-roll the
  guard draw, the partition or the ×2).
- `shapeFor` — a ready boss → VAULT, a short sitting → RUN, a plan-sized queue → JOB12, else JOB.
  **The week rules (D = 2, D ≤ 1, the 22:00 close) are J11's and are deliberately not here.**

## 5. Deviations and objections (the spec was implemented; these are recorded, not fixed)

### 5.1 The ×2 is a vector over TARGET INDEX, so the board prints a count, not a per-contract mark

G3.6 defines the ×2 as `cyrb53(dateISO | jobIndex | targetIndex)` and G3.7 proof 1 requires it to
multiply both branches. A target's **index** does not exist until the draft is made, so a contract
row cannot say which of *its* locks is doubled before you draft. G1's illustrative board prints
`D CS-LIN … ×2 on one target` on a contract row; that number is not computable pre-draft from the
published seed.

Implemented per the spec: `x2Marks(day, jobIndex, budget.targets)` is computed once for the shape's
target indices, the board prints the realised **count** (`board.x2.count`, G3.6's own
`×2: 2 targets`), and `draftUnion` stamps `x2` on target *i* of the drafted queue — before the call,
which is the property the design actually loads (G11: "the ×2 is marked *before* the choice").
**Objection recorded: G1's per-contract `×2 on one target` line cannot be reconciled with G3.6's
per-target-index seed. J6 should print the count, not a per-contract mark, unless the seed changes.**

### 5.2 Criticals are capped at the shape's target count, and the overflow is named

A 10-target job cannot contain 16 critical dues, so "every critical in every draft" is arithmetically
impossible above `budget.targets`. `composeBundles` takes the **most overdue** `budget.targets` of
them as `critical[]` (which is what gets replicated and what the acceptance measures) and returns the
rest in `deferred[]`. Deferred criticals stay on the composed Page and stay due — Global rule 5 is
untouched — they simply are not *guaranteed* into tonight's draft. Measured on the corpus: mean 7.94
criticals per board, max 10, so the cap binds only on very heavy review days.

### 5.3 `JOB_MIN_LOCKS = 2` — "available" is not published

G4 response 1 says the board posts `min(5, available)` without defining *available*. Implemented as
`clamp(floor(queue / 2), 1, 5)`: a contract is at least two locks, which is the smallest contract G1
itself prints (`C · FAC2 · 2 locks`). **Request: put it in `data/job.js` as `BOARD.minLocks = 2`.**

### 5.4 Ordinary locks are re-posted on a thin queue (`ordinaryRep`), which the spec does not publish

With a critical in `r` contracts and an ordinary lock in exactly one, a 3-of-5 draft sees only 60 % of
the ordinary locks. On a queue of exactly one job's worth that serves **6 of 10** composed locks and
pushes 4 to a second sitting for no reason. `composeBundles` therefore picks the cheapest
`r₀ ∈ 1…max(3, n−d+1)` whose `C + (P − C)·cov(r₀)` reaches the shape's target count, where
`cov(r) = 1 − C(n−r, d)/C(n, d)` — exactly the same arithmetic as the critical bound, one notch
weaker. Measured: `r₀ = 1` on every rich board in the corpus; `r₀ = 3` on a deliberately thin
10-lock queue, where all ten drafts then serve 10 of 10.

**Consequence worth naming:** when `r₀` reaches the critical bound, every legal draft yields the same
union — the board offers five contracts and no real choice. That is honest (there is exactly one
job's worth of work) and it is flagged as `board.everyDraftIdentical` and visible in the big
`(−N shared)` figure, but **J6 should print a line for it** rather than let the draft look meaningful.

### 5.5 `posted × 2 ≠ postedFor({…, x2: true})` in general

`econ.postedFor` does ONE round of the whole product (notes/J1.md §5.7), so a half-unit price rounds
up once rather than twice: an 11.25 posting is `11` bare and `23` doubled, not 22. The suite asserts
the envelope equals `econ.postedFor` with the mark on, and separately that the doubled value is
within 1 of `2 × posted`. **J6 must read `econ.postedFor` / `econ.carryFor`, never multiply the
envelope's integer.**

### 5.6 A contract's label breaks an exact tie towards a make no other contract is named after

On a board that is nothing but cold RECALL reviews, five contracts drawn from the same replicated
review block honestly have the same dominant make, and `A VOC · B VOC · C VOC · D VOC · E VOC` is a
useless board. The label is always a **top-count** make (asserted: `counts[label] === max`); when two
or more makes tie at the top, the one no earlier contract has taken wins. `overflow` is
`locks − topCount` regardless, so `A · VOC +2` still means what G1 says it means.

### 5.7 The ramp yields to `sameSkillRun`, 28 times in 500

G1 says both hold "exactly as they hold on the flat Page"; on the flat Page the published ramp rule is
*"hard items never first; a Rematch is never item 1"* plus a general 1→4 ordering, and `spreadSkills`
is the hard constraint (it will drop an item rather than break the run). J5 implements the stronger
reading — a genuinely monotone tier sequence — and yields it to the run cap when the two cannot both
hold (G10 #19 names `sameSkillRun` as COMPOSED's). Measured 472/500 monotone, 28/500 provably
impossible, 0 avoidable. A worked case is pinned in the suite: four tier-1 locks of one make against
three higher-tier locks of three other makes.

### 5.8 `buildJob` returns the sixteen persisted keys with `queue`, `draft` and `board` non-enumerable

`IN_PROGRESS_KEYS` has no room for the answered queue (it lives in `inProgress.queue`, which the
study layer already owns) but the caller needs both from one call. `buildJob` therefore defines
`queue`, `draft` and `board` as **non-enumerable** properties, so `Object.keys`, `JSON.stringify`,
`structuredClone` and `assert.deepStrictEqual` all see exactly the sixteen keys, and
`jobQueueOf(game)` / `jobBoardOf(game)` read them. Asserted both ways.

### 5.9 `page.js` now statically imports `data/job.js` and `js/job/econ.js`

`composeBundles` needs `econ.postedFor` and `BOARD`; `jobBudget` needs `SHAPES` and
`econ.shapeTable`. Restating any of those numbers inside `page.js` is exactly what BUILD-POLICY and
G7 forbid, so the imports are static. That adds **≈ 80 KB** (`data/job.js` 41.6 KB + `job/econ.js`
38.8 KB) to the static graph of `page.js`, hence of `plan.js`, `screens/run.js`, `screens/binder.js`
and `js/job/board.js`. `js/gen/asn-reason.js` was already in that graph through `data/templates.js`.
**Home still imports `page.js` dynamically** (`tests/home-r2.test.mjs:147` is green), so S9 #1's cold
open is not on this path — but **J13 should re-measure the pass-2 import cost**; `postBoard` itself
costs **0.52 ms** mean, all ten drafts priced.

### 5.10 `postBoard(save, today, opts)` takes a third argument

G7 publishes `postBoard(save, today)`. `today` alone cannot carry `now`, the pinned `seed`, the
`jobIndex`, a pre-composed `page` or J7's `tellFor`, so a third `opts` argument was added and `today`
may itself be the opts object. Both forms are asserted.

### 5.11 Exports beyond the three the ticket names

`page.js` also exports `jobTargetOf`, `isCriticalTarget`, `postedCountFor`, `draftCountFor`,
`criticalReplicationFor`, `coverageOf`, `JOB_BUNDLE_IDS` and `JOB_MIN_LOCKS`. Every one is a helper of
the three named functions, additive, and exported so the suite can assert the arithmetic directly
rather than infer it. `x2Marks` was written in `page.js` and **moved to `board.js`** so that
`page.js`'s addition is exactly the three functions and their helpers.
