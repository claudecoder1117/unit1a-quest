## 0. THE HEADLINE — three findings, in order of how much they matter

### F1 (MAJOR, live in the shipped app) — **the tell never retires, because nothing calls `resolve()`**

`js/job/index.js` implements G12 #14 exactly: `resolve()` sets `cleared` and `econ.tellFor` drops to
1.00 that instant; three clean resolutions on three distinct days seal the tag for good. All of that
is asserted and **passes** at module level.

**But no module under `site/js` ever calls `index.resolve()` or `index.trigger()`.** The three
importers of `js/job/index.js` call only read functions:

```
site/js/screens/job.js  : tellFor
site/js/screens/run.js  : indexProgress | sealedOf
site/js/screens/stats.js: areaRollup | backchecksOf | indexProgress
```

`save.game.tags` is therefore never written by the running app, so `recordOf(save, tag).cleared`
stays `false` for ever. Since `tellDetail` filters candidates on exactly that flag, **a tag that
`save.errors` has triggered pays ×1.25 permanently** — which is precisely the "permanent `tell` keyed
on the mere presence of a tag" that G11 rejects and G12 #14 says it closed. The Fault Index
collection (68 cells, the certificate) also cannot progress: nothing can ever seal.

Measured payback, so the size of the hole is on the record: on the cheapest honest target a live tell
is worth a quarter of that target's carry, so **68 deliberate misses repay in ≈ 272 targets** — about
four 70-target weeks. Inside one week the tanker still loses (see §2 row 12), which is why the
ticket's own acceptance line passes; from roughly week five onward a deliberately-tagged save is
ahead for ever.

*In the suite:* `job-exploit.test.mjs` → `J9 · G12 #14` → **`the Fault Index tell is WIRED …`**, a
failing `node:test` **`todo`**. It runs, it fails, it prints the three importers and what they call,
and it does not turn the suite red. A companion test (`the scanner works …`) proves the scanner can
see a real call site, so the failure is not a broken matcher.

### F2 (MAJOR, spec-level) — **G3.7 proof 2's monotonicity claim is false as stated**

> *"all 2ⁿ outcome vectors for n ≤ 11 under optimal play are monotone in clears"*

`m_chain` is in **both** branches of G2 — `miss: Δloose = −min(LOOSE, round(L · m_chain · P · wing_pen))`
— so a clear that deepens the chain raises the price of **every later miss** by `0.2 · L · P · wing_pen`
per step. When the cleared target is cheap and a later missed one is expensive, the amplification
exceeds what the clear paid, and neither BAG (a 10 % fee on the whole pile) nor WALK buys it back.

The four-target counterexample is frozen in `job-monotone.test.mjs` and asserted to the loot unit:

| target | tier · call · wing | outcome | Δloose | chain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | T4 · 85 · open | clean | **+662** | 0 → 1 |
| 1 | T1 · 50 · guarded | **miss** | 0 (P = 0) | 1 → **0** |
| 2 | T3 · 70 · guarded | miss | −46 (m = 1.0) | 0 |
| 3 | T4 · 95 · open | miss | −350 | 0 |
| | | | **616 banked** | |

Flip target 1 from miss to **clean** — the student got it *right* — and:

| target | outcome | Δloose | chain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **clean** | **+5** | 1 → **2** |
| 2 | miss | **−64** (m = 1.4) | 2 → 0 |
| 3 | miss | −350 | 0 |
| | | **603 banked** | |

A correct answer worth **+5** cost **+18** on the next beat. Optimal play (every BAG/PUSH vector plus
CRACK/WALK at the getaway) cannot repair it: the walk line is the optimum on both sides, 616 vs 603.

**Prevalence, measured, not asserted:**

| domain | flip pairs | violations | rate | worst drop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the shipped shapes (RUN 6 · JOB 10 · VAULT 7), honest calls | **46 080** (138 240 at 24 seeds off-suite) | **0** | 0 % | — |
| arbitrary orders, `n = 1…11`, honest calls | 40 962 | **96** | **0.234 %** | 22 loot = **1.9 %** of the bag |
| arbitrary orders, `n = 4…9`, honest calls | 12 240 | **8** | **0.065 %** | 10 loot = **0.8 %** of the bag |

So the claim **holds on everything the game actually deals** and fails on the general statement. The
suite asserts the true, scoped version (green) and ships the general one as a failing `todo`.

The spec's own caveat is real and is worth exactly what it says it is worth: over a matched window of
**7 152 flip pairs**, a frozen always-push policy breaks monotonicity **75** times and the revisable
policy (BAG/PUSH at every beat + CRACK/WALK at the getaway) breaks it **0** times. Revisability is
therefore doing almost all of the work — it just does not do all of it.

*In the suite:* `job-monotone.test.mjs` → `J9 · the exception …` → **`G3.7 proof 2 as stated — ALL 2ⁿ
outcome vectors for n ≤ 11, arbitrary orders`**, a `todo` that prints `96 of 40962 … worst drop 22`.

### F3 (MINOR) — two priced-wrong edges, both pinned

* **A mid-job BAG is still offered on the LAST target's payout**, where `push` ends the job and
  `endJob`'s `bankOnExit` banks the same pile for **free**. Bagging there is a strictly dominated
  10 % fee. Nobody *gains* by it, so it is a trap rather than an exploit — but a student who has
  learned "bag when the pile is big" pays for the habit exactly once per job. Pinned in
  `job-exploit.test.mjs` → proof 6 → `OBSERVED (reported): a mid-job BAG is still offered …`.
  → **Request to J6** below.
* **Half-up rounding inflates the tell on tier-1 targets.** `round(6 × 1.4) = 8` but
  `round(6 × 1.25 × 1.4) = 11`: a realised **×1.375**, not ×1.25. Bounded (≤ ×1.40) and pinned so it
  cannot grow. It makes deliberate triggering *slightly* better than the arithmetic in G12 #14
  assumes, which matters only in combination with F1.

## 1. What was built

Two test files and nothing else. Both are DOM-free, import only from `site/`, and draw every random
object through `js/rng.js` (`rngFrom`) per BUILD-POLICY §2.

### `tests/job-monotone.test.mjs` — proof 2's outcome lattice

The file defines what "optimal play" means **before** it uses it, because the answer decides the
result. An *order* is a realised rung sequence whose calls, tiers, scopes, colds, tells, tokens,
guard flags and crew ranks are already fixed — everything decided before the answer (the sealed
envelope, G1) is part of the order and only the outcome flips. The decisions the student still holds
after seeing an outcome are exactly two:

* **BAG or PUSH** after every target but the last — `econ.optimalOrder` brute-forces this;
* **CRACK or WALK** at the getaway, before the last target — **`econ.optimalOrder` does not model
  this**: it always cracks.

So the file adds `bestWalk()` (bank LOOSE at FULL value via `econ.getawayBank`, forfeit the +10 %
completion bonus because targets remain, exactly as `state.endJob` does for `OUTCOMES.WALKED`) and
takes the max. Two tests pin that model against the shipped machine, so it can never become a private
economy:

* a scripted 12-target job through `state.js` banks **exactly** what `playOrder` says it banks;
* `state.getawayOf().walkBanks === econ.getawayBank(loose) === round(loose)`, and `state.walk()`
  actually banks that.

### `tests/job-exploit.test.mjs` — the other ten proofs

One `describe` per proof, in G3.7's order, plus a twelfth for G12 #14. Every simulation runs on the
shipped modules — `econ.settle`, `call.windowPush`, `crew.drawRung`, `mastery.updateSkill`,
`schedule.applyOutcome`, `xp.scopeFor`, `index.trigger/resolve`, `board.postBoard`, `state.*` — never
on a local re-implementation of a formula.

**The write spy (proof 11)** is the one piece of new machinery. `state.guardSave` *throws* on a Ledger
A write, which proves `state.js` forbids itself; the ticket asks for a spy, i.e. a **measurement** of
what the whole layer actually did. So `spyOn(save)` is a recording deep `Proxy` that refuses nothing
and writes down every `set` / `defineProperty` / `deleteProperty` path at any depth. A full job is
driven through it, then `crew.allocate/withCrew/legalize`, `index.trigger/resolve/mint/spend`,
`board.postBoard/vaultFor`, `guard.guardDist/wingValues`. Result: **370+ writes, 83 distinct paths,
0 matching `^(xp|skills|errors|forecastLog)` or `^cards.*.(bucket|rarity|foil|foilProgress|history)`**,
and the only top-level keys written are `player`, `game`, `inProgress`, `counters`. A companion test
plants `proxy.xp = 9999` and `proxy.cards[id].bucket = 5` and asserts the same matcher catches both —
so the zero is a measurement, not a blind spot.

## 4. Open issues

1. **F1 blocks the Fault Index entirely**, not just the tell: with no `resolve()` caller nothing can
   ever seal, so `index-25` / `index-68` (J7's trophy predicates) and the completion certificate are
   unreachable. Someone owns the wiring; see §5.
2. **F2 is a spec question, not obviously a bug.** Three possible resolutions, none of which J9 may
   make: (a) accept it and change G3.7 proof 2 to the scoped claim this file proves; (b) drop
   `m_chain` from the miss branch (G2's formula), which makes the chain a pure upside and changes the
   whole §3.2 threshold table; (c) cap the miss branch's `m_chain` at the chain value *entering the
   run of clears*. (a) is the cheapest and is what the shipped shapes already satisfy.
3. **`econ.optimalOrder` under-models the getaway.** It always cracks, so its `value` is not the true
   optimum for any order whose walk line is better — which is common when the vault is a T4 at a high
   call. `regretLine` is built on it, so the debrief's regret figure can *overstate* the student's
   loss. `job-monotone.test.mjs` carries `bestWalk()`; folding it into `econ.js` is J1's call.
   → Request to J1.
4. **The tier-1 tell rounding (F3)** is pinned at ×1.375; it is not worth a code change on its own,
   but if J1 ever rounds per-factor rather than once at the end, the pin will catch the drift.
5. **`job-monotone.test.mjs` costs ~12 s**, ~7 s of it the failing `todo` sweep. If the suite's wall
   clock matters more than the report, the `todo`'s seed count can drop from 2 to 1 (the violation is
   at seed 0, `n = 11`) for ~4 s.
6. **Not covered by J9** (they belong to other tickets and are already asserted there): the exact
   `C(5,3)` × 50-save critical-coverage matrix (J5), the `x̂` stake-weighting farm (J3), the crew
   4×2 matrix parameters (J4), the split measurement (J8).

## 6. Deviations from COMPOSED-GAME.md

1. **G3.7 proof 2 is implemented as written and it FAILS.** The suite asserts the true scoped version
   (green) and ships the spec's unqualified version as a failing `node:test` `todo` that prints its
   own counterexample. Nothing was softened: the four-target order is frozen in the file and every
   number in it is asserted exactly (616, 603, −13, +5, +18, ×1.4 vs ×1.0).
2. **G12 #14's tell retirement is implemented in `index.js` and unreachable in the app.** Asserted at
   module level (green) and as a wiring `todo` (failing). The ticket's acceptance line — *"a save that
   deliberately triggers all 68 tags earns LESS over a simulated week"* — passes anyway, for the
   measured reason that 68 misses do not repay inside 70 targets, not for the reason the spec gives.
3. **"Optimal play" was extended beyond `econ.optimalOrder`** to include CRACK/WALK at the getaway,
   because `state.js` offers it and `optimalOrder` does not model it. Without the extension there are
   7 further violations of ≤ 1 loot that are artefacts of the model rather than of the game. The
   extension is pinned against the shipped machine by two tests, so it is not a convenient fiction.
4. **The ticket's "200 mastered-tier-1 jobs" runs as a target-by-target economic simulation** through
   `econ.settle` / `call.windowPush`, not through `state.js`: forcing `composePage` to deal 2 000
   mastered tier-1 targets 200 times over would be a fixture of the composer, not of the brake being
   tested. Both arms of the comparison use the same driver, the same time model
   (`ANSWER_MINUTES_PER_TIER` + `DECISION_SECONDS`) and the same rung bands.
5. **The mastery-tanking week holds the crew budget EQUAL between the arms.** An earlier framing that
   gave the tanker free STEADY on every make had the tanker winning 5/5 — which measures the gift, not
   the tanking. Both arms now spend the same 8 points under the same greedy rule. This is a
   methodological choice the ticket did not specify and it changes the answer, so it is recorded here.
6. **"Total weekly loot monotone in compliance" is modelled with a FIXED page size** (dues lead, the
   rest new material) rather than "dues only". Under a dues-only model a non-complier answers fewer
   targets and the claim is trivially true; under a fixed page the non-complier answers the same
   number of targets at worse multipliers, which is the claim worth proving. Noted because the
   horizon matters: the ordering is strictly monotone at 7 and 14 days and **inverts at 28 days**
   (0.75 → 5 519 beats 1.0 → 5 343) — full compliance advances buckets and deflates its own pool,
   which G2 owns explicitly (*"posted falls as you master the material. That is the point."*). The
   spec says *weekly*; at weekly it holds.
